Square FP-ori: exact coordinates, rigidity, envelope and nonintersection
====================================================================

Construction attribution
------------------------
Fengrui Liu, Tatsuro Terakawa, Siying Long and Masaharu Komori,
"Rigid-foldable cylindrical origami with tunable mechanical behaviors",
Scientific Reports 14, 145 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-50353-4.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10762141/

This is an independently checked coordinate realization of the square,
alpha=45-degree case of their FP-ori construction. It is not a claim to
have invented FP-ori. The explicit envelope and nonintersection proofs
below are deductions from these coordinates, not quotations from the paper.

Model and cuts
--------------
Take a square prism of side length l and initial height l. On each square
side face add both diagonals: crease the lower-left-to-upper-right diagonal
and cut the other diagonal. At the intersection of the two diagonals the
cut separates the center into two material points. Four rigid right
isosceles triangles remain on each side. Vertical prism edges and the
edges joining side triangles to the top/bottom squares are hinges.

The ideal model has zero-thickness panels and perfect line hinges. A slit
of zero or small initial width opens into a finite gap during the motion.
Thus this is a cut-sheet mechanism, not a sealed tube. The full cut must
reach its stated endpoints; leaving bridges across it adds constraints.

Coordinates
-----------
It is sufficient to take l=1; multiply every coordinate by l otherwise.
Let 0 <= theta <= pi/2, c=cos(theta), s=sin(theta), h=sqrt(c), u=sqrt(s),
and define

  D = (1+u)^2+c,
  r = c/D,
  p = 2*h*(1+u)/D,
  q = ((1+u)^2-c)/D.

Let Q be the 90-degree rotation about z and Rot(theta) the theta rotation.
For k=0,1,2,3, with indices modulo four, define

  B_k = Q^k (1/2, -1/2, 0),
  T_k = Rot(theta) B_k + (0,0,h),
  J_k = Q^k (r,0,p/2),
  I_k = Q^k Rot(theta) (r,0,0) + (0,0,h-p/2).

Each original prism side has the four triangles

  D_k = (B_k, B_(k+1), J_k),
  L_k = (B_k, T_k, J_k),
  U_k = (T_k, T_(k+1), I_k),
  R_k = (B_(k+1), T_(k+1), I_k).

Creases are the shared edges B_k J_k and T_(k+1) I_k, in addition to
vertical/prism-ring hinges. Cut edges are B_(k+1) J_k, B_(k+1) I_k,
T_k J_k and T_k I_k. J_k and I_k coincide in the initial prism but
are distinct material points thereafter.

Exact panel rigidity
--------------------
The identities p^2+q^2=1 and

  2*h*p - (c+s-1)*q = c-s+1

follow by substitution. In the second identity the remaining numerator
is 2*c*(s-u^2)=0. Also |B_k-T_k|^2=1 because h^2=c.
These identities give, for every triangle listed above, side lengths

  (1, 1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(2)).

Thus every facet is a fixed nondegenerate triangle, not merely a set of
fixed outer-ring edges. Shared hinge edges coincide identically because
their two panels use the same endpoints. This proves continuous finite
rigid folding over the entire stated interval. No triangle loses area
even at the flat endpoint; the area of each triangle is 1/4.

Constant radius and full axial collapse
--------------------------------------
The top and bottom vertices have radius R=1/sqrt(2) at every theta.
The extra vertices have radius r=(1-q)/2 <= 1/2 < R. Every panel is a
convex hull of its vertices, so all of every panel lies inside the same
solid circular cylinder of radius R. This is an exact constant outer
envelope, although internal vertices leave the cylindrical surface.

Further,

  h-p = h*(s+c-1)/D >= 0,

with strict inequality for 0<theta<pi/2. Hence every side-panel vertex
lies between z=0 and z=h. At theta=0, h=1 and J=I=(1/2,0,1/2) per side,
giving the original square prism. At theta=pi/2, h=p=r=0 and q=1;
the two squares coincide after a 90-degree rotation and all facets are
flat. Intermediate slit-center separation is

  |I_k-J_k|^2 = 2*r^2*(1-c) + (h-p)^2 > 0.

Nonintersection for every interior state
---------------------------------------
The following is an analytic proof for zero-thickness panels. It does
not rely on sampled collision detection. It permits the intended shared
hinges and vertices. At the final flat state, face-to-face overlap is the
intended limiting stack.

1. The four bottom triangles D_k occupy disjoint azimuthal sectors
   [k*pi/2-pi/4, k*pi/2+pi/4], apart from the common boundary rays.
   Each extreme ray meets a bottom triangle only at its corresponding
   B vertex. Thus bottom triangles only meet at their shared vertices.
   The same statement holds for the top triangles U_k after rotation.
   All D_k have z<=p/2; all U_k have z>=h-p/2. Since p<h in the open
   interval, bottom and top triangles are strictly separated in height.

2. Group the vertical triangles into pairs (L_k,R_(k-1)), one pair for
   each vertical edge B_k T_k. A pair lies in the azimuthal interval

   k*pi/2 + [min(-pi/4,theta-pi/2), max(0,theta-pi/4)].

   Its width is max(theta,pi/2-theta), strictly below pi/2. Therefore
   different vertical-edge pairs lie in disjoint sectors. The projected
   vertices lie in a convex cone of angular width below pi, so their
   entire triangles lie in that same angular cone. Within a pair the
   panels are noncoplanar, as the determinant in step 4 is positive;
   therefore they meet only on their common edge B_k T_k.

3. Consider D_0 and vertical triangles. Angular sectors eliminate all
   possibilities except L_0, L_1, R_0 and R_3. L_1 meets its sector
   only on the ray through B_1, so only the intended vertex is common.
   L_0 shares the crease B_0 J_0 with D_0. They are noncoplanar for
   interior theta, since the determinant in step 4 is nonzero.
   For R_0, use the plane of D_0 with normal N=(p,0,q). Its vertices
   B_1,T_1,I_0 have the signed plane values

     N dot (B_1-B_1) = 0,
     N dot (T_1-B_1) = h*u*(c+1-u^2)/D > 0,
     N dot (I_0-B_1) = 2*h*u^3*(1-u^2)/D^2 > 0.

   Thus R_0 meets the plane of D_0 only at B_1.

4. For D_0 versus R_3, use the plane of L_0, with normal

     n = (T_0-B_0) cross (J_0-B_0).

   Direct substitution, using c^2+u^4=1, gives

     n dot (B_1-B_0) = -h*u/2 < 0,
     n dot (I_3-B_0) = h*(1+u)*(c+u-u^2)/D^2 > 0.

   D_0 and R_3 lie on opposite sides of this plane. D_0 meets the
   plane on B_0 J_0; R_3 meets it on B_0 T_0. These edges have only
   their common endpoint B_0, proving the required separation.

5. Rotation by multiples of pi/2 proves the same statements for every
   D_k. The spatial isometry in cylindrical coordinates

     (radius,azimuth,z) -> (radius,theta-azimuth,h-z)

   exchanges bottom and top triangles and exchanges the two vertical
   triangle types. It therefore proves every remaining U_k case.
   Top and bottom square caps, if present, meet side triangles only
   at their intended edges/vertices, since other vertices have strictly
   intermediate heights.

This exhausts all triangle pair types, proving no unintended intersection
for 0<theta<pi/2. The endpoints follow directly from the explicit initial
prism and the continuous flat-stack limit.

Verification
------------
verify_fp_proof.py uses exact SymPy polynomial reduction modulo
h^2=c and c^2+u^4=1. It checks all 48 triangle edge-length identities,
the four separation determinants, the p<=h identity and the slit gap.
The proof of positivity uses 0<u<1 and c,h,D>0; it is not inferred from
floating-point samples. This proves existence of the prescribed symmetric
motion. It does not assert that a free physical assembly has only one
degree of freedom without guides or an actuation constraint.
