Exact triangular cell: rigidity, containment, and continuous nonintersection

This is the diagonal-slit FP-ori topology with a rectangular, not square,
side face. All panels have zero thickness. Flat endpoint contact is allowed.
The proof covers the complete motion, including rigid triangular end caps.

1. Cut pattern and coordinates

Let R>0, a=R/2, b=sqrt(3)*a, and delta=2*pi/3. Each of the three extended
side faces is a rectangle of width 2b=sqrt(3)*R and height 2a=R. Cut one
complete diagonal; hinge the other diagonal in its two half segments.
Each full diagonal has length 2R. The two free copies of its midpoint are
J and I. A zero-width slit is ideal; no bridge may remain across the cut.

Write Q(t) for rotation about the vertical axis. For 0<=theta<=pi/3, put

  C=cos(theta), S=sin(theta), d=1-C,
  v=sqrt(d+sqrt(3)*S), E=2-2*d+2*v+v^2,
  h=2*a*sqrt(1-2*d),
  q=(2*d+2*v+v^2)/E, p=h*(1+v)/(a*E), r=a*(1-q).

The vertices, with k modulo 3, are

  B_k=Q(k*delta)(a,-b,0),
  T_k=Q(k*delta+theta)(a,-b,0)+(0,0,h),
  J_k=Q(k*delta)(r,0,a*p),
  I_k=Q(k*delta+theta)(r,0,0)+(0,0,h-a*p).

Each face has four triangles:

  D_k=(B_k,B_(k+1),J_k), L_k=(B_k,T_k,J_k),
  U_k=(T_k,T_(k+1),I_k), F_k=(B_(k+1),T_(k+1),I_k).

The slit lips are B_(k+1)->J_k->T_k and B_(k+1)->I_k->T_k.
The end triangles remain rigid, parallel, and coaxial. The deployed height
is R and the flat twist is pi/3. The end triangles differ by this rotation
in the flat plane; they do not have to coincide vertex for vertex.

2. Exact panel isometry

Let X=a*(C-1)+b*S=a*(v^2-2*d), Y=a*S+b*(1-C), and
D=2*a-(b/a)*Y. Elementary substitutions give

  p^2+q^2=1,
  h^2+4*R^2*sin(theta/2)^2=4*a^2,
  D=h^2/(2*a)-X,
  h*p-X*q=D.

For example, the last identity follows on substituting p and q above;
its numerator cancels using h^2=4*a^2*(1-2*d). The rim-triangle lengths are
|B_k B_(k+1)|=2b and |B_k J_k|=|B_(k+1) J_k|=sqrt(a^2+b^2)=R.
The vertical triangle has |B_k T_k|=2a=R; expanding |T_k J_k|^2 and using
the last identity makes it a^2+b^2=R^2. Thus L_k is equilateral throughout.
Reflection (radius,angle,z)->(radius,theta-angle,h-z) proves the other two
triangle types have the same fixed lengths. This is a finite exact motion.

3. Radial and axial bounds

In the open interval 0<theta<pi/3,

  0<d<1/2, 0<v<sqrt(2), 0<q<1, 0<r<a.

The first two follow directly from theta's range; q<1 follows because
E-(2*d+2*v+v^2)=2*(1-2*d)>0. The ring vertices have radius R, and all
other vertices have radius r<R. Convexity of the radius-R disk puts every
point of every triangle inside the cylinder; the maximum radius is R.

Also sqrt(3)*S>=3*d, because S/d=cot(theta/2)>=sqrt(3). Consequently
X=a*(sqrt(3)*S-d)>0 and

  a*p/h=(1+v)/E<1/2,

since E-2*(1+v)=X/a>0. Bottom triangles lie at or below a*p and top
triangles at or above h-a*p, so those two families are strictly separated.
All other vertices, and therefore all panels, lie between the end planes.

4. Angular reduction of possible intersections

Each D_k projects into the sector k*delta+[-pi/3,pi/3], meeting either
bounding ray only at its rim vertex. Their interiors cannot intersect.
The same applies to the U_k family after rotation by theta.

The pair (L_k,F_(k-1)) lies in the sector

  k*delta+[theta-delta,0].

Its width delta-theta is strictly less than delta and less than pi, so
it is a convex angular cone. These sectors are disjoint for different k.
Within a pair the planes are distinct, as shown in section 6, and the two
triangles meet only on their common B_k T_k hinge.

For D_0 the only vertical triangles requiring examination are
L_0,L_1,F_0,F_2. L_1 meets D_0's sector only on the B_1 ray, and the
only shared point is B_1. Sections 5-6 handle the other three triangles.
Rotational symmetry and the reflection in section 2 then cover every
panel pair, including all comparisons involving the top triangles.

5. D_0 versus F_0

The plane of D_0 has normal N=(p,0,q) and passes through B_1. Direct
substitution gives these signed distances (N need not be a unit normal):

  N dot (T_1-B_1) = h*(2*d+2*v-v^3)/E > 0,

  N dot (I_0-B_1) = h*G/E^2 > 0,
  G=2*v*(sqrt(3)*S-2*d*C)+2*d*(2-d+sqrt(3)*S).

For the first inequality 2*d>0 and v*(2-v^2)>0. For the second,
sqrt(3)*S>=3*d>2*d*C, and the remaining factors are positive.
Thus F_0 meets D_0's plane only at their shared vertex B_1.

For an explicit algebra check of the second identity, its numerator
before using the circle identity is

  G=4*d^2*v-6*d*v-2*d+v^4+2*v^3.

Using v^2=d+sqrt(3)*S and S^2=2*d-d^2 gives the positive expression above.

6. D_0 versus L_0 and F_2; separation within a vertical pair

Let m=(T_0-B_0) cross (J_0-B_0). Then

  m dot (B_1-B_0) = -2*a*b*(h*q+X*p) < 0,

  m dot (I_2-B_0) = 2*sqrt(3)*a^2*h*P/(3*E^2) > 0,
  P=9-12*d+(12-4*d)*v+3*v^2-2*v^3.

Every grouped term of P is positive: 9-12*d>3, 12-4*d>10, and
3*v^2-2*v^3=v^2*(3-2*v)>0 since v<sqrt(2)<3/2.

For a reproducible derivation of the second determinant, substitute

  m=(a*p*Y-b*h, -a*(h*q+X*p), b*X+a*q*Y),
  I_2-B_0=(r*(-C+sqrt(3)*S)/2-a,
             r*(-S-sqrt(3)*C)/2+b, h-a*p).

Replace C by 1-d, S by (v^2-d)/sqrt(3), p by h*(1+v)/(a*E),
and q by (2*d+2*v+v^2)/E. Reduce the numerator by

  v^4-2*d*v^2+4*d^2-6*d=0,

which follows from C^2+S^2=1. The resulting expression is exactly the
positive determinant displayed above. verify_triangle.py checks this
identity and all panel distances with exact symbolic arithmetic.

The negative first determinant shows D_0 and L_0 have distinct planes;
they meet only on their common B_0 J_0 hinge. D_0 and F_2 lie on opposite
sides of L_0's plane. They meet that plane respectively on B_0 J_0 and
B_0 T_0, whose only common point is B_0. The positive second determinant
also proves L_0 and F_2 are noncoplanar, justifying the vertical-pair claim.

7. Endpoints and caps

At theta=0, h=R, q=0, p=1 and J_k=I_k: the four triangles tile each
rectangular side of the embedded triangular prism. At theta=pi/3,
h=p=r=0 and q=1: every panel is flat inside the radius-R disk. All
intermediate states have the strict separations proved above. Cap planes
cannot cross side-panel interiors because their non-rim vertices have
strict heights between 0 and h. The ideal final lamination may have
contact and overlap; finite thickness requires a nonzero stopping height.
